Toolkit #3 · Behaviour · Any subject

The first 5 minutes.

A calm start that runs itself: five steps, one Do Now template, and the research behind them.

Why it matters. The start of a lesson sets the tone for the rest of it. When students know exactly what to do as they walk in, learning starts before you say a word, and you avoid the “settle down…” loop. The EEF recommends simple, consistent classroom routines, taught explicitly, as one of the most practical ways to improve behaviour.

The 5-step routine

  1. 1

    Meet them at the door

    Stand at the door. Greet each student by name, with a smile or a nod. Add a short reminder for anyone who needs it.

    Say: “Good morning. Do Now is on the board.”

  2. 2

    Same Do Now, same place

    Have the Do Now on the board before students arrive, in the same place every lesson. They should be able to start without asking what to do.

    Tip: keep a slide titled “Do Now” as slide 1 of every lesson deck.

  3. 3

    3 quick retrieval questions

    One from last lesson, one from last week, one challenge. No new content: everyone should be able to start. About 4 minutes, working on their own.

    Then: go through the answers in 1 minute. Students check and correct their own.

  4. 4

    Teach the routine like content

    Explain it, model it, let the class practise it, and give feedback. Re-teach it after holidays, or whenever it slips. Routines feel effortless only after they are taught.

    Say: “Let's practise coming in again. Show me what a strong start looks like.”

  5. 5

    Praise out loud, correct quietly

    Narrate what's going right so everyone hears the expectation. Deal with anyone off task privately, at their desk, with the least fuss.

    Say: “Row 2 have already started question 1. Thank you.”

Make it stick

Do

  • Keep the same routine in every lesson, for weeks
  • Use a visible timer for the Do Now
  • Make question 1 easy, so everyone succeeds early
  • Agree the routine with your team, so it's the same across classes

Avoid

  • New content in the Do Now
  • Talking over students while they work
  • Correcting in public when a quiet word works
  • Dropping the routine when the class seems settled
